01 / 05the problem

No account, no cloud, nothing to pay: Whisper runs on the Mac itself, through whisper.cpp.

The hard part is typing while the speaker is still talking.

Whisper revises earlier words as it hears more, and a word already typed into another app cannot be quietly taken back.

Re-reading the whole utterance on every pass also slows down the longer the speech runs.

02 / 05how it works

A word is typed once two passes in a row agree on it, so streaming never backspaces.

The Python prototype came first. The Swift menubar app ports its streamer with a smaller window and greedy decoding, since beam search crashed whisper.cpp mid-dictation. What follows is the Mac app.

A global hotkey, option-space by default, starts capture, resampled to 16 kHz mono. Every 350 ms the recent audio goes to whisper.cpp’s base.en model, which returns one word per segment, each with a timestamp.

Typed words reach the focused app as Unicode key events. Only “scratch that” erases, and only what the current dictation typed.

Little silence reaches the model. A window under 0.5 s gets no pass, a near-silent one is skipped, and trailing quiet is cut to 0.2 s, since Whisper fills silence with phrases like “thank you”.

Once more than 3.0 s of committed audio sits in the window, the window restarts 1.5 s before the last committed word, so each pass costs about the same however long the speech runs. In toggle mode, 1,500 ms of silence ends the dictation.

03 / 05swallowed words

Words went missing mid-sentence.

The commit rule kept a new pass’s words only if they started after the last typed word ended. whisper.cpp re-times words by up to a second from one pass to the next, so words that both passes heard fell through the gap. On JFK, “can” moved to start 0.4 s before the end of the typed “you” and was discarded.

The fix (commit 262c910) finds the boundary between typed and new words by matching text instead of trusting the times. On 20 held-out LibriSpeech speakers, streaming had dropped 78 of 523 words before it. Streamed word error fell from 19.7% to 6.7%, against 5.4% offline.

04 / 05results

On all 41 public clips, streaming scores 5.8% word error against 4.4% for offline decoding of the same audio, and JFK streams with 0 errors.

Commit lag, how long after a word ends it is typed, has a median of 787 ms at the 350 ms tick, against 1,184 ms at a 600 ms tick, the Python app’s old one. A word needs two agreeing passes, so the tick sets most of it. A pass decodes in 165 ms at the median. Timing was measured on an M5 with other work running, so it is an upper bound.

Swift tests, all passedon CI, with 36 Python tests over the same fixtures

CI builds the release app and replays the benchmark’s gate clips through it with the real model; it fails if streaming word error exceeds offline by more than 2 points, or if the JFK clip loses a word. A control run with a broken window must fail, and does on both counts: 14.3%, with 2 JFK words lost.

Not shown: other models or languages, since the benchmark runs whisper.cpp base.en, an English-only model; other kinds of speech, since its 41 clips are public read speech; and an idle machine, since the lag was timed with other work running.
