01the problem

A local, free stand-in for Wispr Flow.

Whisper runs on the machine through whisper.cpp, with no account, no cloud and $0 a month. Streaming is the hard case. Whisper revises earlier words as it hears more, and a word already typed into another app cannot be quietly taken back. Re-reading the whole utterance on every pass also slows down the longer the speech runs.

02how it works

The Python prototype came first. The Swift menubar app ports its streamer, with a smaller window and beam search. The walk-through below follows the mac app. A global hotkey, option-space by default, starts capture, converted to 16 kHz mono. Every 350 ms the window of recent audio goes to whisper.cpp’s base.en model, which returns one word per segment, each with a timestamp. A word is typed once two consecutive passes agree on it, so the backspace count is always 0. Committed words reach the focused app as Unicode key events.

Little silence reaches the model. A window under 0.5 s gets no pass, a near-silent one is skipped, and trailing quiet is cut to 0.2 s, since Whisper fills silence with phrases like “thank you”. Once more than 3.0 s of committed audio sits in the window, the front is dropped and 1.5 s kept for context, so each pass costs about the same however long the speech runs. In toggle mode, 1,500 ms of silence ends the dictation.

03swallowed words

Words went missing mid-sentence.

A diagnostic log separates capture shortfall from model skip. It compares wall time with captured audio to catch capture drops, and flags any gap over 0.7 s between committed words whose audio still carries speech energy, RMS above 0.02, as a model skip. The window was the cause. At 8.0 s of committed audio with 2.0 s kept, whisper.cpp fell behind real time and its greedy decoder skipped phrases, about 2 s at a time; the next trim made each loss permanent. The mac app now trims at 3.0 s, keeps 1.5 s and decodes with beam search.

Trimming has a seam of its own. whisper.cpp’s timestamps are coarse, so after a trim the last committed word can come back at the head of the next window, just past the time filter. A text check over the last 6 words drops the repeat. A test pins it: “world” heard again at 0.95 s is dropped and “again” goes through.

04results

The Python core has no model or microphone in it, so its 29 tests run without either. All of them passed on CI, covering the commit rule, the boundary repeat, append-only output and the trimmed window. The Swift mac app has no test target, so its smaller window and beam search are not under test. The repo holds no latency, word-error or battery measurement.
