01the problem

A local, free stand-in for Wispr Flow.

Whisper runs on the machine through whisper.cpp, with no account, no cloud and nothing to pay. Streaming is the hard case. Whisper revises earlier words as it hears more, and a word already typed into another app cannot be quietly taken back. Re-reading the whole utterance on every pass also slows down the longer the speech runs.

02how it works

The Python prototype came first. The Swift menubar app ports its streamer with a smaller window and greedy decoding, since beam search crashed whisper.cpp mid-dictation. What follows is the mac app.

A global hotkey, option-space by default, starts capture, resampled to 16 kHz mono. Every 350 ms the recent audio goes to whisper.cpp’s base.en model, which returns one word per segment, each with a timestamp.

A word is typed once two passes in a row agree on it, so streaming never backspaces. Typed words reach the focused app as Unicode key events. Only “scratch that” erases, and only what the current dictation typed.

Little silence reaches the model. A window under 0.5 s gets no pass, a near-silent one is skipped, and trailing quiet is cut to 0.2 s, since Whisper fills silence with phrases like “thank you”.

Once more than 3.0 s of committed audio sits in the window, the window restarts 1.5 s before the last committed word, so each pass costs about the same however long the speech runs. In toggle mode, 1,500 ms of silence ends the dictation.

03swallowed words

Words went missing mid-sentence.

The commit rule kept a new pass’s words only if they started after the last typed word ended. whisper.cpp re-times words by up to a second from one pass to the next, so words that both passes heard fell through the gap. On 20 held-out LibriSpeech speakers, streaming dropped 78 of 523 words.

The fix, 262c910, finds the boundary between typed and new words by matching text instead of trusting the times. Streamed word error fell from 19.7% to 6.7%, against 5.4% offline. The window and decoder were chosen on the other 20 speakers and JFK; the held-out speakers were first scored after the fix was committed. A bigger 8.0 / 2.0 s window still lost words (15.5% word error), and so did no trimming at all, so the window was not the cause.

04results

On all 41 public clips, streaming scores 5.8% word error against 4.4% for offline decoding of the same audio, and JFK streams with 0 errors.

A word is typed a median 0.79 s after it ends at the 350 ms tick, against 1.18 s at the first version’s 600 ms. A pass decodes in 165 ms at the median. Timing was measured on an M5 with other work running, so it is an upper bound.
