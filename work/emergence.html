01the problem

Wireworld’s rule fits in one line: a conductor fires when 1 or 2 of its neighbours are heads.

Climbing from that rule to a processor that runs programs takes signals, gates, arithmetic and memory, with every rung verified. The climb is the last of eight acts in one canvas engine, from Langton’s ant to Wireworld.

02how it works

The engine keeps shared typed-array grids, and each act supplies only its own step and seed functions. Wireworld has 4 states: empty, head, tail and conductor. A head moves one cell per tick along a conductor, and the tail behind it keeps the wire one-way.

Two cells are built in that raw rule. The NAND cell is a 5-cell junction fed by a clock loop. When a and b both arrive with the clock pulse, 3 heads meet and the junction stays dark. The loop register holds a bit as an electron circling a loop, written through a diode, read by a tap and cleared by a timed pulse.

The CPU is a SAP-1 extended with stores and branches: 8-bit data, 4-bit addresses and 16 words of memory. It is one flat netlist of the two cells. Each clock, every NAND settles in topological order, then every flip-flop samples its input and all of them commit at once.

03the asynchronous wall

Hand-wired Wireworld gates failed when composed.

A long lead on one input corrupted the other, and the wires between gates carried signals backward until they oscillated. The fix is the one silicon uses: latch every signal in a flip-flop once per clock and let the NAND logic settle in between. NAND is functionally complete and the flip-flop holds state, so two primitives, a NAND gate and a D flip-flop, cover the whole machine.

The Wireworld loop register needs the same timing. A clear pulse that meets the circulating electron leaves 0 electrons in the grid. The same pulse 2 ticks late leaves the bit readable.

04results

Five test programs run against expected output: 5 + 3 and 9 − 4, a countdown, a running sum, and Fibonacci until 8-bit overflow trips the carry flag.

  • 1,446NAND gates in SAP-1one gate-level netlist, not yet compiled whole into Wireworld
  • 175flip-flopsRAM, 16×8, holds most of them
  • 18,895NAND gates in SAP-2and 2,118 flip-flops, its 256-byte memory included

SAP-2 adds a stack and runs multiply, divide, GCD, primes and bubble sort. It is one netlist of 18,895 NAND gates and 2,118 flip-flops, its 256-byte memory included. A separate build in Conway’s Life, a level below Wireworld, makes gates from glider collisions. Its routed half-adder runs in the raw rule; run once per bit, with the sum and carries passed between runs, it adds 511+1=512. The CPU does not run on it.
