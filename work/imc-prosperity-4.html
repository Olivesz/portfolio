01the problem

Each team submits one Python Trader class, and the simulator calls its run method every tick with the full limit-order book for each product.

Round 2 has two products. ASH_COATED_OSMIUM oscillates around 10,000; INTARIAN_PEPPER_ROOT trends. Each is capped at ±80 units. One holds its level and the other climbs, so a fair value that suits one trails the other.

02how it works

Four steps run per product per tick: estimate a fair value, take any resting order priced beyond an edge from it, quote both sides near it, and store the estimate for the next tick.

The fair value blends two readings of the book. The microprice averages the best bid and ask, each weighted by the size on the other side, so it leans toward the thinner side. The wall mid averages the top two levels on each side by volume, so a 2-lot bid that steps eight ticks ahead of a 27-lot wall moves it by about a quarter of a tick, while the plain mid jumps four. The blend, 75% wall for Osmium and 55% for Pepper, feeds an exponential moving average whose state travels between ticks as a JSON string.

Inventory moves the quotes. They center on a reservation price, the fair value shifted against the position. A long position lowers it, shrinks the buy size and narrows the edge for selling into bids, so the book pulls the position back toward flat. No order can push the position past ±80.

03one model per product

Each product gets its own fair-value model.

Pepper opens day −1 at 11,001.5 and closes day 1 at 13,999.5, and an exponential average of a climbing price trails it. The Pepper model subtracts a fixed drift of 0.001 per timestamp, smooths what is left with a weight of 0.03 and adds the drift back. Osmium’s average starts at 10,000.5 and takes a weight of 0.05 from each new reading. Pepper’s quotes also lean 60% of the way from the model toward the live book; Osmium’s do not.

04results

The bot backtests to 297,604 seashells on the three Round 2 days.

Two scripts sit around the trader. One runs a coordinate-descent search over seven of the constants on the Round 2 days and keeps whichever value raises total profit. The other scores a whole trader on the full set of days, on each single day and on each pair of days, and adds the worst day and the worst pair to the total. The backtest above runs on the same days, so it is in-sample.

The trader needs nothing beyond the Python standard library. Sixteen tests cover limit safety, taking, quoting, skew and state, and run in CI on Python 3.11 and 3.12.
