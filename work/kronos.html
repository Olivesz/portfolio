01the problem

Market data never comes with the answer, so a fitted estimator can be wrong and still look right.

KRONOS is a regime-aware research platform on about 48 liquid US equities and ETFs, 2010–2026, every estimator hand-written on NumPy, pandas and SciPy. One rule runs through it: no estimator touches a real price until it recovers a planted answer on synthetic data. 41 gates hold that rule, 39 on synthetic worlds with the answer planted and 2 calibrating against the real market. Each synthetic gate shows size and power: silence on a null world, detection on a world with the effect built in.

02how it works

Market returns feed a three-state Student-t hidden Markov model (bull, volatile, bear), refit walk-forward every 21 trading days. Trading reads only filtered probabilities, each computed from data through that day. A regime flips after 5 straight days above 0.65, and no sooner than 10 days after the last flip unless the new state passes 0.90.

The overlay sizes the whole book: the lever times whichever brake is tighter.

03the sign bug

Realized volatility sat at 6.9% against a 13% target.

Tracing where the return went found the drawdown throttle inverted. Its multiplier sat at 0.50 at the high-water mark, rose to fully open by a −8% drawdown and stayed open through −20%, the wrong side hidden by a clip at 1.0. It braked on 93.6% of days, at a mean multiplier of 0.64, and none of the 30 gates then in the suite tested its direction.

The repair was pre-registered with kill criteria before the repaired system ran, and shipped with gate X27: the multiplier holds at 1 down to a −8% drawdown and falls linearly to a 0.25 floor at −20%. The fix alone lifted CAGR from 6.4% to 8.8%. The same audit found that a plain min() of lever and brakes could never exceed 1, so the volatility target was out of reach; the lever became a multiplier, with financing charged on the levered part. Financing took about 37% of the levered variant’s CAGR gain, under the 50% kill line. The old numbers stay in the repo as the baseline row.

04results

In-sample, 2013–2026, after costs, with up to 1.5× leverage, against SPY over the same days. SPY still wins on raw return. The universe is today’s liquid names, so the backtest carries survivorship bias.
