01the problem

Market data never comes with the answer, so a fitted estimator can be wrong and still look right.

KRONOS is a regime-aware research platform on about 48 liquid US equities and ETFs, 2010–2026, every estimator hand-written on NumPy, pandas and SciPy. One rule runs through it: no estimator touches a real price until it recovers a planted answer on synthetic data.

47 gates hold that rule. Most test both ways: silence on a world with no effect, detection on a world with the effect built in. CI runs every one of them on the committed real prices, with nothing skipped, and again on a synthetic market. On the real prices the suite also checks:

  • the battery’s calibration on SPY X19
  • the regime engine on the 2020 crash and the calm of 2017
  • range-based volatility on SPY X1
  • the backtest’s accounting
  • the price snapshot’s integrity X39
  • the headline itself, recomputed on every run X40

02how it works

In the regime-gated book, market returns feed a three-state Student-t hidden Markov model (bull, volatile, bear), refit walk-forward every 21 trading days. Trading reads only filtered probabilities, each computed from data through that day. A regime flips after 5 straight days above 0.65, and no sooner than 10 days after the last flip unless the new state passes 0.90.

The regime weights its signals: 12-1 momentum, reversal and low volatility. A hierarchical-risk-parity backbone with a Black-Litterman tilt, which feeds the signal into the weights as a view, builds the book.

The overlay sizes the whole book: the lever times whichever brake is tighter. The regime-gated book lost to plain equal weight (1/N) under a pre-registered test, so it is kept as the control every new construction must beat. The shipped book is equal weight under the same overlay, with a 21-day momentum tilt.

03the sign bug

Realized volatility sat at 6.9% against a 13% target.

Tracing where the return went found the drawdown throttle inverted. Its multiplier sat at 0.50 at the high-water mark, rose to fully open by a −8% drawdown and stayed open through −20%, the wrong side hidden by a clip at 1.0. It braked on 93.6% of days, at a mean multiplier of 0.64, and none of the 30 gates then in the suite tested its direction.

The repair was pre-registered with kill criteria before the repaired system ran, and shipped with gate X27: the multiplier holds at 1 down to a −8% drawdown and falls linearly to a 0.25 floor at −20%. The fix alone lifted CAGR, the compound annual growth rate, from 6.4% to 8.8%.

The same audit found that a plain min() of lever and brakes could never exceed 1, so the volatility target was out of reach; the lever became a multiplier, with financing charged on the levered part. Financing took about 37% of the levered variant’s CAGR gain, under the 50% kill line. The old numbers stay in the repo as the baseline row.

04results

The shipped book holds about equal weight’s Sharpe (return per unit of risk) at 64% of its drawdown, and beats SPY’s Sharpe at 56% of its drawdown.

These are in-sample results over the same days, 2013–2026, after costs and with up to 1.5× leverage. That is risk control, not alpha: equal weight and SPY still win on raw return, and on Sharpe over cash the book lands just short of equal weight, which the repo reports as a failure. The universe is today’s large caps, picked with hindsight, so the backtest carries survivorship bias.

Two checks run outside the backtest (fig 5). Since 2026-08-17 a CI job has logged the unlevered KRONOS-TRADE variant, not the shipped book, before each session; after 35 trading days its gap to SPY is about one standard error, too soon to confirm or refute the backtest. A second stream records the shipped book’s own decisions from 2026-10-14.

Frozen with every US setting and rerun on Japan, Europe and Asia-EM, the shipped book kept roughly half the local index’s drawdown, but equal weight on the same names beat its Sharpe in all three markets, and the index did in Japan and Europe.
